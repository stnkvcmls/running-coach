Mockups for docs/UI_UX_CRITIQUE.md. Each row shows the current screen on main (default skin, seeded data, 23 June 2026) beside the proposal. Pink tags are change IDs from the document; they are not part of the design.

Today, before the run

The screen answers one question first: what am I running, and is my body up for it? Everything else moves below or into other tabs.

  • X1The week strip appears only here, as a rail that also shows each day's planned session, so it does real work instead of sitting inert on every tab.
  • X2"Week 26/53" becomes the training block: "Build block, week 1 of 4".
  • X3 X4Theme toggle and the empty sync pill leave the top bar. Theme follows the system; sync problems surface only when something is wrong.
  • T2The hero names the workout in the coach's notation and draws its structure, coloured by effort.
  • T3One primary action. The briefing is a sentence inside the hero, not a second card with a second purple button.
  • T4Check-in drops from about 330px to about 190px: one row per question, with the end labels under the scale.
  • T6 T7Body numbers come with a comparison: Garmin's own status for HRV, your 90-day range for the rest. Training load becomes one sentence and a two-line chart.

Today, after the run

Today the 10:20 interval run shows up at the bottom under "Also today", while the hero still offers to send the same workout to the watch. The proposal matches the run to the plan day and turns the hero into the result.

  • T1A run of the planned type on the planned day completes the session, with or without a Garmin calendar event. If the match is ambiguous, ask once: "Was this today's intervals?"
  • T1The rail fills Tuesday in and the week total updates to 9.2 of 73 km. Plan shows the same state (P6).
  • D5The rep chart replaces the 13-row adherence table as the first read: six bars against a target line.
  • D7The effort rating is asked here, right after the run (decided). The run page keeps it for edits.

Plan

Three week navigators (global strip, week tabs, day cards) collapse into one. The page reads as a schedule: day, session, distance, state.

  • P1No global week strip. The week selector is the only week control, and each tile shows the week's shape, not just a km figure.
  • P2Preferences and Regenerate move into the overflow menu. They are occasional actions and shouldn't sit above the plan.
  • P7Season phases become one monochrome ribbon with a "you are here" mark. The five-colour legend goes.
  • P3Send to watch appears on the next session only. Notes show only when they say something specific to that day.
  • P4One effort dot per row instead of a coloured border, a coloured badge and a glyph all saying the same thing.
  • P6Today's completed run counts toward the week (9.2 of 73 km), the same as Today.

Run analysis

For a structured session the question is "did I hit the reps?". The page answers that first, then explains it through heart rate and effort.

  • D2One coach verdict at the top, with the full analysis behind a link. Today the same text appears twice: once as a chip, once as a card at the bottom.
  • D1 D5"Description" with a bare structure bar, plus a 13-row adherence table, becomes one rep chart with pace and HR for each rep.
  • D3Time in zone shows minutes on the page, not only in a hover tooltip, and uses the same effort colours as the rest of the app.
  • D4The HR chart gets a distance axis, rep shading and a y-axis that starts at a sensible floor instead of zooming 160 to 170.

Progress

Wellness numbers only mean something against your own normal. Each trend shades your usual range and says in words whether today is inside it.

  • G1The y-axis is 28px wide, so "100" renders as "00" on Sleep score and Body battery. Small multiples here have no y-axis; the band and the value say enough.
  • G2Five charts in five different colours become one ink. Colour is kept for effort.
  • Q3HRV's band and verdict come from Garmin's own baseline and status, so the app and the watch agree. The other four use your middle 80% of the last 90 days.
  • G3Six tabs (Wellness, Performance, Intensity, Aerobic, Records, Custom) become three: Body, Fitness, Records. Custom charts move to the end of Fitness.

Settings

Today, Settings is one 4,600px scroll of fourteen sections. The proposal is an index of grouped rows, each showing its current value and opening its own page.

  • S1Group by what the athlete is trying to do: you, connections, coach, app, data.
  • X3Appearance (System, Light, Dark) lives here, not in the top bar. System is the default.
  • X4Garmin sync status gets a real row with its last-sync time; the top-bar pill goes.
  • S2The zones editor gets its own page, wide enough that zone names aren't cut to "Rec", "Aer", "Ten".
